## Fixed comparison protocol

- The independent 2025 performance holdout is outside this comparison. The extraction query has an exclusive `2025-01-01` upper bound. Existing 2025 coverage observations are not used as a selection signal.
- Only result history, race identity, and entry identity enter the comparison. C4 (`C4_NOT_READY`), C3 exhibition information, motors, and odds are absent from the extraction and rating formulas.
- All races on business day D are scored from one state containing results through D-1. Each D race's update is computed against that same state; day-level changes become visible on D+1. The current race's `actual_course` is used only after the result for its rating update. Known `boat_no` is distinct from `actual_course`.
- Common performance scoring uses the same complete, strict six-boat races for all supported candidates. This avoids changing the test population when comparing training eligibility. Consequently, these metrics describe performance conditional on complete numeric-finish races; they do not establish calibration on races with F/L or other special outcomes. 2023 chronological folds tune the bounded candidate grid; 2024 is the development period's out-of-time comparison. No random split is used.
- Primary diagnostics are race-level first and exact-second log loss for their respective series. The bounded grid uses their equal-weight mean to select a shared configuration within each family; Brier score, calibration, and top-1 hit rate are additional diagnostics. Overall and short-term ranking scores are examined separately. Differences too small or unstable to justify added complexity favor the simpler method.

## Eligibility proof and limits

**STRICT (S).** Require `RESULT_RECORDS_PRESENT`, six canonical result rows, six `NUMERIC_VALID` finishes, unique numeric ranks, no unresolved result, and, for course-series updates, an `actual_course` permutation of 1–6. This is the complete-ranking baseline.

**ROW-SAFE (R).** For pairwise updates only, require a race with six known entries and `result_state = 'RESULT_RECORDS_PRESENT'`; use only pairs of distinct `NUMERIC_VALID` finishers. The finish-state view invalidates every numeric boat in a duplicate-rank race, so that race supplies no such pairs. The comparison `rank(i) < rank(j)` is invariant to removal of every other boat, including one with a special finish. A special, F, or L boat receives no numeric finish score or inferred placement. Pairwise updates are normalized within each race in one candidate variant, so a race with many valid pairs need not have proportionally greater total weight. Evaluation remains on STRICT races. A six-way softmax or complete Plackett–Luce likelihood cannot use this partial ranking without changing its target or assigning a relation to special boats, so R is unsupported for those families.

**PARTIAL (P).** No implementation is permitted for a missing or unresolved race record. Numeric pairs in such races may appear ordered, but source completeness and finality are unproven. P therefore remains an explicitly unsupported candidate, rather than a synthetic rank or silently broadened R.

## Newcomer and availability rules

The established newcomer initial value is the existing valid players' per-series mean multiplied by 0.8; simultaneous confirmed newcomers on D must share the mean computed before any of them is inserted. `CONFIRMED_NEWCOMER` requires evidence available by D of both cohort membership and first-race status. The current retrospective KI/cohort observations do not establish that as-of evidence for the 2023–2024 first-observed players. Such players remain `NEWCOMER_UNCERTAIN` and receive the ordinary unknown-player initializer, with no retrospective 0.8 discount. A known existing player is `NON_NEWCOMER`.

The 2017–2024 source results are preserved retrospectively, but their original publication/availability timestamps are not established by the current source. The D-1 mechanical guard can be tested; historical as-of availability remains a separate limitation. A retrospective-development method freeze can preserve that uncertainty, but must not be described as verified historical as-of or live prediction readiness.

## Candidate definitions

**A — course softmax.** Maintain separate player ratings for first place and exact second place at each actual course 1–6, giving 12 series. Before a race, estimate a course mixture from only earlier days' venue × known boat-number to actual-course counts, with one pseudocount in each course. For each boat, take the mixture-weighted rating and apply a six-boat softmax. This is a defined score estimator, not exact marginalization over unknown courses. After all D races, use their observed actual courses to update the corresponding series from first-place and exact-second one-hot targets. Compare learning rates 8 and 16, with 2017–2022 warm-up versus explicit 2023 reset. Use STRICT updates.

**B — pairwise Elo.** Maintain one overall player rating. For every eligible numeric pair in a race, use an exponential-logistic expected win probability at rating scale 400 and add K × (observed − expected). Compare K=8 and 16 with per-pair weight, K=8/16/120 with race-normalized weight, both initialization policies, and STRICT versus ROW-SAFE. The K=120 normalized variant checks the effective scale of K=8 per-pair on a complete 15-pair race. Probabilities for first and exact second come from a six-boat softmax/Plackett–Luce marginal using the same rating scale. Every day's pair deltas are computed from D-1 and applied after D.

**C — online Plackett–Luce.** Maintain one overall player rating. For a complete numeric ordering, sum sequential-choice gradients over the remaining boats, apply learning rate 8 or 16 after D, and score first and exact second from coherent race-level marginals. Compare warm-up and reset with STRICT updates. Partial rankings are unsupported.

**SHORT_50 comparator.** For each player, maintain a maximum-50 preceding-entry window or preceding-STRICT-valid-result window. Transform an observed strict finish position r to `(3.5 − r) / 2.5`; a nonstrict entry may occupy the entry window with no score. The prior-window mean maps to `1000 + 100 × mean` for a standalone six-boat probability diagnostic. For fewer than 50 observations, compare available-history mean, a fixed D-1 B overall-rating fallback, career finish mean, and initialization. The standalone diagnostic tests the definition of the short series; it does not prove incremental value when used beside the other 13 series.

## Comparison results and choice

The v2 comparison artifact contains **28 supported A/B/C candidates, 8 explicitly unsupported A/C ROW-SAFE candidates, and 8 short-window comparators**, including every supported candidate's parameter set, eligibility, initialization, 2023 and 2024 metrics, counts, per-candidate compute time, and quarterly stability. Its SQL reads 2,671,344 ordered 2017–2024 boat rows in one read-only repeatable-read snapshot. All supported candidates use 51,246 common STRICT scoring races in 2023 and 51,392 in 2024; 4,746 and 4,672 other races, respectively, are excluded from scoring. The 2023 selection statistic is Q2–Q4 prequential combined first/exact-second log loss after Q1 development; 2024 is out-of-time development validation.

A is best on combined log loss in every 2024 quarter. Its 2024 first/exact-second Brier scores are 0.609120/0.802135, 10-bin calibration errors are 0.021145/0.008576, and first-place top-1 hit rate is 0.561391. B STRICT yields first/exact-second Brier 0.771367/0.815218 and 10-bin calibration errors 0.012590/0.010069. The full bin-level calibration tables, discrimination measures, and quarter metrics for **all** candidates are in the JSON; the first/second probabilities each sum to one by construction.

The overall series selects B STRICT. ROW-SAFE supplies 55,325 eligible 2024 update races versus 51,392 for STRICT, but improves combined validation log loss by only 0.000480 per scored race. Its small gain does not justify a broader update population for v1. B is simpler than C and has better first/exact-second combined loss; C has somewhat better full-ranking likelihood (6.106903 versus 6.122104), so the overall choice favors the later first/second prediction objective, not a claim that B dominates every ranking metric. K=120 race-normalized STRICT is numerically identical to K=8 per-pair STRICT because each eligible race has 15 pairs. The matched-scale comparison removes the misleading K=8/16 normalized-versus-per-pair learning-rate disparity.

2017–2022 warm-up outperformed explicit 2023 reset for the selected A (2024 combined LL 1.489451 versus 1.592431), B (1.668118 versus 1.669696), and C candidate (1.672600 versus 1.674395). Warm-up uses only earlier history; no pre-2017 results are assumed. No rating decay is selected. The A and B rating temperature/scale is fixed at 400; learning rate 16 for A and race-normalized K=120 for B are the selected parameters.

The best 2024 standalone short comparator was ENTRY/AVAILABLE_HISTORY at 1.771360. VALID_RESULT/AVAILABLE_HISTORY was 1.771368, a difference of 0.000008 with quarter differences of mixed sign. v1 selects VALID_RESULT because a window of strictly eligible results has a simpler, consistent meaning. It uses the available prior 1–49 results rather than future filling; at zero results it keeps the initializer. The 2024 strict-scoring entries comprise 305,639 with 50 valid prior results, 2,655 with 1–49, and 58 with none. The fixed overall-rating fallback and initializer-only alternatives performed worse; all eight alternatives remain in the JSON. This short rating is retained as the required fourteenth feature, with its incremental value in a later combined prediction model still unproven.

## Leakage, reproducibility, and freeze

The comparison code hard-bounds the extraction at `race_date < DATE '2025-01-01'` and rejects any returned later row. Targeted tests cover the D-1 state, two same-day races, current-race course perturbation, partial race exclusion, coherent probabilities, F/L-adjacent short-window behavior, and simultaneous confirmed newcomer means. The v2 run was read-only / repeatable-read, and its candidate metrics and four quarterly counts reconcile to the same 2024 scoring population. After the v2 run, the previously unexercised confirmed-newcomer SHORT_50 initializer branch was implemented and tested; its real-data branch count was zero, and observed-case evaluation paths did not change. Seventeen targeted tests pass. Before that branch-only addition, the repository suite had 134 passing tests and 70 opt-in database tests skipped.

The authoritative **Rating Method Freeze v1** is `artifacts/rating_method_freeze_v1.json`. It fixes 12 A course series, one B overall series, one STRICT valid-result SHORT_50 series, their eligibility, initialization, day boundary, parameters, the 2024-12-31 development cutoff, and dataset/code/artifact SHA-256 values. The machine-readable comparison is `artifacts/rating_method_comparison_2023_2024_v2.json`; the earlier `.local/rating_method_comparison.json` is preserved as superseded exploratory evidence. The v2 run took 834.64 seconds; selected-candidate accumulated processing times were approximately 21.68 seconds for A, 14.97 for B, and 20.63 for SHORT_50. The remaining total covers shared extraction, hashing, other candidates, and serialization. These times describe this local run, not a production throughput guarantee.

**Verdict: `RATING_METHOD_FROZEN` for the retrospective method specification.** This does not certify historical publication-time availability, live prediction readiness, or incremental value of SHORT_50. The saved results are conditional on six numeric finishes; they do not characterize calibration on special-result races. No production rating-state migration was made. In the next stage, one independent 2025 holdout performance run may use this exact v1; any changes after seeing 2025 outcomes require a distinct v2 and must not relabel the same holdout as untouched.
